CORRECTIVE RAG (CRAG) INTERVIEW GUIDE
====================================




1. WHAT IS CRAG?

CRAG (Corrective Retrieval-Augmented Generation) enhances traditional RAG by evaluating retrieved documents before sending them to the LLM.

Goal:
Detect poor retrieval results and correct them before generation.

ARCHITECTURE

User Query
     |
     v
 Retriever
     |
     v
 Retrieved Documents
     |
     v
 Retrieval Evaluator
     |
     +--> High Confidence
     |         |
     |         v
     |      Generate Answer
     |
     +--> Medium Confidence
     |         |
     |         v
     |   Retrieval + External Search
     |
     +--> Low Confidence
               |
               v
         External Search
               |
               v
          Generate Answer
		  
		  
Why CRAG Is Needed
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Goal:
		Verify whether retrieved documents are actually useful
		for answering the query.

	Solution:
		Introduce a Retrieval Evaluator.


Retrieval Evaluator
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Purpose:
		Evaluate relevance of retrieved documents.

	Inputs:
		- User Query
		- Retrieved Documents

	Output:
		- Relevance Score
		- Decision

	Possible Decisions:
		1. Correct
		2. Incorrect
		3. Ambiguous
		
		

G. Correct 
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Condition:
		Retrieved documents are highly relevant.

	Process:
		Retrieved Documents
			↓
		Knowledge Refinement
			↓
		Generation

========================================================================

H. Incorrect 
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Condition:
		Retrieved documents are not relevant.

	Process:
		Query
			↓
		Web Search
			↓
		External Knowledge
			↓
		Generation

========================================================================

I. Ambiguous 
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Condition:
		Documents are partially useful.

	Process:
		Internal Knowledge
			+
		External Knowledge
			↓
		Combined Context
			↓
		Generation



M. Retrieval Evaluation Logic
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Inputs:
		- Query
		- Retrieved Document

	Output:
		Relevance Score (0 to 1)

	Meaning:
		1.0 = Perfectly Relevant
		0.0 = Completely Irrelevant

========================================================================

N. Threshold-Based Decision Making
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Lower Threshold:
		0.3

	Upper Threshold:
		0.7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Correct:

		Condition:
			At least one document score > 0.7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Incorrect:

		Condition:
			No document score > 0.3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Ambiguous:

		Condition:
			Between both thresholds.

========================================================================

O. Good Documents
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Definition:
		Documents with score greater than lower threshold.

	Purpose:
		Only these documents are used for generation.

========================================================================
		

IMPORTANT INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

Q1. What is CRAG?
Ans: Corrective Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a framework that evaluates retrieval quality and applies corrective actions when retrieval is poor.

Q2. Why was CRAG introduced?
Ans: Traditional RAG heavily depends on retrieval quality. Incorrect retrieval results can cause hallucinations.

Q3. Main component of CRAG?
Ans: Retrieval Evaluator.

Q4. What does Retrieval Evaluator do?
Ans: Measures confidence and relevance of retrieved documents.

Q5. Confidence categories in CRAG?
Ans:
- High Confidence
- Medium Confidence
- Low Confidence

Q6. What happens during high confidence?
Ans: Retrieved documents are refined and directly used.

Q7. What happens during low confidence?
Ans: Retrieval results are discarded and external search is performed.

Q8. What happens during medium confidence?
Ans: Retrieved documents are combined with additional search results.

Q9. What is Decompose-and-Recompose?
Ans: A refinement approach where useful information is extracted and noise is removed before generation.

Q10. Advantages of CRAG?
Ans:
- Better retrieval quality
- Lower hallucinations
- Improved robustness
- Better response accuracy

Q11. Disadvantages of CRAG?
Ans:
- Additional latency
- More infrastructure
- Extra retrieval cost

Q12. Difference between CRAG and Traditional RAG?
Ans: RAG trusts retrieved documents. CRAG validates retrieval quality before generation.

Q13. Difference between CRAG and Self-RAG?
Ans: CRAG focuses on correcting retrieval quality. Self-RAG focuses on self-reflection and generation validation.

Q14. When should CRAG be used?
Ans: Large enterprise knowledge bases, compliance systems, financial systems, and frequently changing knowledge sources.

Q15. In one line explain CRAG.
Ans: CRAG evaluates and corrects retrieval results before the LLM generates the answer.
