1. WHAT IS SELF-RAG?

Self-RAG (Self-Reflective Retrieval-Augmented Generation) is an advanced RAG framework where the LLM can decide:
- Whether retrieval is needed
- When to retrieve
- How to evaluate retrieved information
- Whether generated answers are supported by evidence

Goal:
Improve factuality, reduce hallucinations, and make retrieval dynamic.

B. Problems with Traditional RAG
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	1. Unnecessary Retrieval

		Problem:
			RAG performs retrieval even when retrieval is not needed.

		Example:
			Question:
				How many seconds are there in a minute?

		Issue:
			LLM already knows the answer.
			Retrieval creates unnecessary overhead.

	------------------------------------------------------------

	2. Blind Trust in Retrieved Documents

		Problem:
			Traditional RAG assumes retrieved documents
			are always relevant.

		Risk:
			Incorrect documents can produce incorrect answers.

	------------------------------------------------------------

	3. No Answer Verification

		Problem:
			Generated answers are not checked.

		Risk:
			Hallucinations may reach users directly.


========================================================================

C. What is Self-RAG?
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Definition:
		Self-RAG introduces self-reflection into the
		retrieval and generation pipeline.

	Core Principle:
		Continuously evaluate every step.

========================================================================

D. Four Key Questions Answered by Self-RAG
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	1. Is Retrieval Needed?

		Question:
			Can the LLM answer directly using
			parametric knowledge?

	------------------------------------------------------------

	2. Are Retrieved Documents Relevant?

		Question:
			Do retrieved documents actually help
			answer the query?

	------------------------------------------------------------

	3. Is the Generated Answer Grounded?

		Question:
			Does every claim in the answer come from
			the retrieved evidence?

	------------------------------------------------------------

	4. Is the Answer Useful?

		Question:
			Does the answer really solve the user's question?

F. Retrieval Decision Stage
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Purpose:
		Determine whether retrieval is necessary.

	Case 1:
		General Knowledge Question
			→ Direct Generation

	Case 2:
		Company-Specific Question
			→ Retrieval Required

========================================================================

G. Document Relevance Filtering
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Purpose:
		Remove irrelevant documents.

	Process:
		1. Retrieve documents.
		2. Evaluate each document.
		3. Keep only relevant documents.
		4. Remove noise.

	Result:
		Relevant Documents List

========================================================================

H. Example of Relevance Filtering
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Question:
		Who is the CEO of Nexa AI?

	Retrieved Documents:
		4 Documents

	Relevant Documents:
		1 Document

	Outcome:
		Only the CEO-related document is retained.

========================================================================

I. Answer Generation from Context
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Purpose:
		Generate answers only from relevant context.

	Inputs:
		- User Question
		- Relevant Context

	Output:
		Answer

========================================================================

J. Support Verification Stage
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Purpose:
		Detect hallucinations.

	Question Asked:
		Is the generated answer supported by evidence?

========================================================================

K. Support Categories
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	1. Fully Supported

		Meaning:
			Every statement comes from retrieved evidence.

	------------------------------------------------------------

	2. Partially Supported

		Meaning:
			Some facts come from evidence.
			Others are fabricated by the model.

	------------------------------------------------------------

	3. Not Supported

		Meaning:
			Answer is mostly or completely hallucinated.

========================================================================

L. Hallucination Example
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Question:
		What are the side effects of Drug X?

	Evidence:
		- Dizziness
		- Nausea

	Generated Answer:
		- Dizziness
		- Nausea
		- Fatigue
		- Headache

	Observation:
		Fatigue and headache are not supported.

	Result:
		Partially Supported

========================================================================

M. Answer Revision Stage
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Purpose:
		Remove unsupported facts.

	Input:
		- Original Question
		- Context
		- Generated Answer

	Task:
		Rewrite answer using only supported facts.

========================================================================




N. Revision Loop
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Generated Answer
		↓
	Support Check
		↓
	Partially Supported / Not Supported
		↓
	Revise Answer
		↓
	Support Check Again

	Repeat Until:
		Fully Supported

========================================================================

O. Retry Protection
------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Problem:
		Infinite revision loops.

	Solution:
		Maximum Retry Count

	Example:
		Max Retries = 5

	After Limit:
		Return No Answer Found

IMPORTANT INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

Q1. What is Self-RAG?
Ans: A RAG framework where the model retrieves information only when required and uses self-reflection to verify its answers.

Q2. Why was Self-RAG introduced?
Ans: To reduce hallucinations and improve answer quality by allowing the model to evaluate retrieval and generation quality.

Q3. Difference between RAG and Self-RAG?
Ans: Traditional RAG always retrieves. Self-RAG decides when retrieval is required and evaluates generated content.

Q4. What are reflection tokens?
Ans: Special control tokens used during training to guide retrieval, critique, and verification decisions.

Q5. Advantages of Self-RAG?
Ans:
- Better factuality
- Dynamic retrieval
- Lower hallucination rate
- Better document utilization

Q6. Disadvantages of Self-RAG?
Ans:
- More complex architecture
- Higher inference cost
- Additional training requirements

Q7. When should Self-RAG be used?
Ans: Research assistants, enterprise search, compliance systems, medical and legal knowledge assistants.

Q8. How does Self-RAG reduce hallucinations?
Ans: Generated responses are checked against retrieved evidence before finalizing the answer.

Q9. What is adaptive retrieval?
Ans: Retrieval occurs only when the model determines external knowledge is needed.

Q10. Is Self-RAG better than basic RAG?
Ans: Generally yes for complex knowledge-intensive tasks, but with additional cost and complexity.


Q. What is decide_retrieval in Self-RAG?
Ans:
It determines whether external knowledge is required.
If not required, the model generates directly.

Q. What is is_relevant?
Ans:
It checks whether retrieved documents are relevant to the query.

Q. What is is_sup?
Ans:
It checks whether the generated answer is supported by retrieved evidence.

Q. What is revise_answer?
Ans:
If the answer is not supported by evidence, the model revises the answer using retrieved documents.

Q. What is is_use?
Ans:
It evaluates whether the answer is useful, complete, and informative for the user.

Q. What is rewrite_question?
Ans:
If the answer is not useful, the query is reformulated and retrieval is performed again.

Q. Why is Self-RAG called Self-Reflective RAG?
Ans:
Because the model continuously evaluates its own retrieval decisions and generated answers before returning the final response.

Self-RAG and CRAG solve different problems.

Self-RAG focuses on LLM self-reflection, while CRAG focuses on retrieval quality correction.

Many people think Self-RAG completely replaces CRAG, but in practice they are complementary.

Q: If Self-RAG performs retrieval checking and answer validation, why do we still need CRAG?

Answer:

Self-RAG primarily focuses on self-reflection during generation. It decides whether retrieval is required, checks document relevance, verifies answer support, and evaluates answer usefulness.

However, Self-RAG does not deeply assess the overall quality of retrieved knowledge. If the retriever returns outdated, incomplete, or incorrect documents, Self-RAG may still generate an answer based on poor evidence.

CRAG solves this by introducing a retrieval evaluator before generation. It measures retrieval quality and can trigger corrective actions such as filtering documents, re-retrieving, or using external search.
